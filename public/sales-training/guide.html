2 of 16 · Frameless

No face frame. That is the whole difference.

An American cabinet has a wooden frame screwed to the front of the box, and the door hangs off that frame. A European cabinet does not. The hinge mounts straight to the side panel.

Why anybody cares

That frame is usually an inch and a half of wood on each side. It sits in front of the opening, so you reach past it every time. Take it away and the opening is as wide as the box.

Roughly fifteen per cent more usable space across a run — and drawers that are genuinely full width, not full width minus the frame.

Say it like this “Ours is European frameless. There’s no wooden frame across the front, so the hinge goes straight onto the side panel and the opening is the full width of the box. You get about fifteen per cent more room, and the doors sit flush in a continuous line.”

We build

  • Frameless only — European full-access
  • Flat-panel (slab) and shaker doors
  • Kitchens, vanities including ADA
  • Walk-in and reach-in closets
  • Mudrooms, credenzas, coffee bars
  • Laundry, offices, media walls
  • Multi-family and commercial casework

We do not build

  • Framed — we stock it, we don’t build it
  • Raised-panel doors
  • Inset doors
  • Solid-wood shaker — ours is machined MDF
  • Countertops
  • A single box taller than 96″
Build and sell are different verbs — you need both We manufacture frameless only. We also stock an RTA framed line we buy in and resell, so “do you do framed?” is a yes — followed straight away by whose it is: “that’s a stocked product we carry; our plant runs frameless.” Volunteer it, like the shaker. Raise framed only when they need it now, want a face-frame look, or the budget won’t reach Essential — and never quote a framed style, size or price from memory. There is no stock sheet yet.

Anatomy of the box.

Six answers live on this page, and a customer will ask for four of them in the first ten minutes.

The three boards

BoardWhere it belongs
PlywoodThe cabinet box. Strong, holds a screw, survives a wet sink base
ParticleboardDoor and panel cores — and most of the closet system. Also the whole box, if a customer needs cost out
MDFDoor cores, especially shaker: machines cleanly, takes paint raw. Some closet components

What a customer is really asking

“Is it real wood?” almost always means “is it cheap?” Answer the real question:

“The box is three-quarter-inch plywood — that’s real wood, and it’s the part carrying the load. The doors are engineered board, deliberately: they stay flat in Houston humidity where solid wood moves.”

Always ¾ inch. Never the metric — this is the US market, and some of our own flyers get this wrong.

The three that get missed

Why 34.5″? A 1½″ countertop lands the finished height at 36″. Say the finished number to a homeowner and the box number to a trade.

Bath base is 21″, not 24″. The single most common dimension error.

96″ is a hard ceiling. Plywood comes in 4×8 sheets, so a taller single box would need a sheet that does not exist. Taller runs stack.

Worth practising Lay out a 12-foot L-shaped kitchen using only stock sizes in 3″ increments. Do it a few times on paper and you will stop needing the table.

Widths run 9″ to 42″ in 3″ increments. Anything outside that is a filler or a custom panel — both are fine, both go on the Discovery Sheet.

The shaker disclosure — the most important thing on this page

A traditional shaker door is five pieces of solid wood: two rails, two stiles, and a panel that floats in the middle so it can move with the season. Ours is one piece of MDF with the shaker profile machined into it, finished in RTF.

A customer who finds this out on their own concludes we hid it. A customer who hears it from you in the first meeting concludes we know what we are doing. It is the same fact. The only variable is who says it first.

Volunteer it, in this order “Our shaker is machined MDF rather than five pieces of solid wood — and that’s on purpose. MDF doesn’t move with humidity, so the door can’t warp or split at a joint the way a wood shaker will, and it takes paint better because there’s no grain. In Houston that matters more than it would somewhere dry.”
Never Never say or imply solid wood about a shaker door. Not “solid feel,” not “solid construction,” not “real wood look” in a sentence about the core.

6 of 16 · Overlay

How much of the box the door covers.

Overlay is set by the hinge and its base plate — so a designer specifying an overlay is really specifying hardware. This is the question designers ask that reps fumble.

InsetDoor sits inside the opening, flush with the front. We don’t make this
Full overlayDoor covers the side panel completely. Kitchens and vanities
Half overlayTwo doors share one panel, each covering half of it. Closets. The diagram calls it partial — same thing

Frameless plus full overlay is what produces the wide openings and the unbroken line across a run. Those are the two things worth saying out loud when somebody asks why frameless.

Why closets are different A closet is a row of units side by side. Half overlay lets two adjacent doors share a single panel between them, so you are not paying for two panels where one does the job.
The connection to remember Overlay is a hardware decision. The hinge cup sits in the door; the base plate sits on the side panel; the distance between them is the overlay. Change the base plate and you change the overlay — which is why “can you do a different overlay?” is a real question with a real answer, not a styling preference.

Four ways a door opens.

OpeningWhat it actually isWhere it fits
HandlesBar pulls, arch pulls, knobs, in three hardware finishesEvery line. The default
J-pullA shaped lip on the edge of the door itself. Handleless look with no separate part to buyHandleless on a budget
GolaA continuous aluminium channel profile set into the carcass. The door stays perfectly plain; your fingers go into the channelReserve and Atelier
Push-to-openA sprung latch. No handle, no channel — press and it releasesSignature and up

Gola is in the box. J-pull is on the door.

A designer will catch you on this one J-pull is not gola. They look almost identical in a photograph and they are different products at different prices: gola is an aluminium extrusion fitted into the cabinet, J-pull is a machined profile on the door edge. We have already mis-captioned one of our own project photos over exactly this. If you are not certain which one a picture shows, say “handleless” and check.
Hardware finishesBrushed nickel · polished chrome · matte black
Gola finishesAnodised silver · matte black · brushed gold on Atelier

Gola is an aluminium extrusion, anodised or powder-coated. It is a finger recess and a door edge — nothing else is built into it.

9 of 16 · Hardware

The same hardware, every line.

Nobody buys a cabinet for its hinge. But a builder who has lost a Friday to doors that will not line up buys the next job on it — so this page is worth more to a trade customer than the finish library.

The hinge — DTC soft-close

AdjustmentSix-way — height, depth and side-to-side, at both leaves
Why that mattersAn installer trues a door up on the wall. No pulling it off the box, no shims, no second trip. Houston walls are rarely square
Soft closeBuilt into the hinge cup. Standard, every door, not an upgrade
MountingStraight onto the side panel — there is no face frame in the way
OverlaySet by the base plate. See page 6

The slide — Knape & Vogt 8450FM

ExtensionFull-extension — the back of the drawer clears the cabinet
Load100 lb class, telescopic ball-bearing
Soft closeForce Management — a rack-and-gear module. Lower peak pull force and no jerk at the release point, unlike a plain spring damper
MountSide-mount, unhanded, lever-release disconnect
MakerKnape & Vogt — Grand Rapids, Michigan, making drawer slides since 1898
Be honest about this one It is a side-mount slide, so you see it when the drawer is open — not a concealed undermount. If a competitor pitches undermount, say so plainly and sell the 100 lb rating and the Force Management close, which are the parts a customer actually feels.
Do not claim Not “made in USA.” KV is an American company with plants in more than one country and we have not verified origin for this slide. Say “an American brand that has been making slides since 1898” — true, and it lands the same way.

11 of 16 · Lighting

Light is what makes a finish look expensive.

A soft-touch matte door in a dim room is a grey cupboard. The same door with a lit underside is the reason somebody chose Reserve. We sell lighting as part of the cabinetry — so bring it up early. It is far easier to design in than to add later.

Where it goes

Under-cabinetUnder the wall run, washing the counter and the backsplash. The most common and the most useful
Display unitsAtelier has integrated lighting on display cabinetry as standard — open shelving, glass fronts, media walls
Closet interiorsDressing Room has integrated lighting as standard. In-shelf lighting keeps shoes and folded stacks visible
VanityBacklit mirrors and lit open towers — see page 15
Toe kick & interiorsAvailable on request across the range, priced per job
Ask this before you show a single finish “How much natural light does that room get?” It changes which finish family you show them, and it opens the lighting conversation without a hard sell.
Why it is worth raising unprompted Lighting is one of the few things a customer can add that changes how the whole job looks for a small fraction of the whole job’s cost. It is also far cheaper to run at build than to retrofit once the cabinets are on the wall.
Lighting has its own session — this page is the sales view You have enough here to raise it, place it and sell it. The technical detail — strip type, colour temperature, dimming and switching, drivers, and how it is priced — is covered properly in a separate lighting tutorial. Until you have sat through that one, put the specifics on the Discovery Sheet: “we spec lighting per job — tell me what you’re picturing and I’ll come back with options and a number.” Do not guess at a colour temperature in front of a designer.

13 of 16 · Closets

The same ladder — built differently.

Know this before you say “same as the kitchens” Closets are built to cabinet standards in the same plant, but they are not the same board. Most of the closet system is particleboard-cored panel — the same core as our door panels. Some components are MDF. If a customer wants a plywood closet we can custom laminate it, but that adds lead time and costs more. Say it that way round: it is available, it is not standard. Never say a closet box is plywood without checking the job.

Three things a closet customer needs to hear

Half overlay. Two doors share one panel. That is the one construction difference from a kitchen, and a designer will ask.

The margin is in the organisers, not the box. An empty carcass is a commodity. The valet rail, the jewellery drawer and the pull-out hamper are not.

Adjustable, not fixed. Shelving moves on pin holes. What they own now is not what they will own in five years.

Never, from memory

  • A per-cabinet price — ballpark by the linear foot, never by the box
  • A deposit percentage — terms are in the written agreement
  • A warranty length — it comes with the agreement
