What do these terms mean?
Framed vs Frameless Cabinets
Frameless Cabinets do not have a face frame on the front of the cabinet. Traditional cabinets have a face frame. We only manufacture Frameless Cabinets.
This is important, because this changes the:
Type of hinges used
Size of the Door
Supports for the Drawer Slides
Assembly and Installation method
Frameless Cabinets (We Make)
Pros:
Sleek, modern look.
More storage space and easier access.
Great for contemporary kitchens.
Cons:
Less sturdy without the frame.
Needs precise installation.
Can be pricier.
Framed Cabinets ( We don't Make)
Pros:
Strong and durable.
Perfect for traditional styles.
Easier to install and more affordable.
Cons:
Less storage space.
Smaller openings can be harder to use.
Can look bulkier in tight spaces.
Door/ Hinge Types: Full Overlay and Half overlay
The type of overlay describes how the door is seated in front of the cabinet. This is determined by the type of Hinge and Hinge Plate used to connect the door to the cabinet box.
Inset Doors have their sides enclosed within the side panels of the cabinet box. (We don't use this)
Full Overlay Doors completely cover the side panels of the cabinet box (We use this for our Cabinets)
Half Overlay Doors only cover the side panels partially. This is good if you want to share a single panel between two cabinets, or have doors on either side of the same panel. (We use this for our Closets)

Plywood, Particle Board, and MDF
Plywood - Strong and Reliable. We use them for the Cabinet Box. They can be finished with a stain, or even melamine/laminate sheets for decorative purposes.
Particleboard - Made out of compressing smaller scraps of wood and sawdust, and is glued together with resin. Typically used as the core for Flat Door Panels. This is the cheapest compared to plywood and MDF, and you cannot carve designs into them, and you cannot paint on raw particleboard. You can make cabinet boxes out of this as a cheaper alternative.
MDF (Medium Density Fiberboard) - Made the same way as particleboard, but using finer wood fibers instead. These are also used as the core for doors. These have the added benefit of coming in different grades, which you can carve out of (To make Shaker profiles onto) and also paint on their raw surface.
All the boards that we use are mostly 3/4" Thick.  ( And 1/4" Thick for specific applications like the Back panels and Drawer bottom panels)
The size of these boards can vary, but typically come in 4ft x 8ft sheets (48" x 96"). This is especially true for Plywood, which we use to make the cabinet boxes. Its for this reason we don't make single cabinets that are taller than 96", because anything bigger would need sheets of plywood that are larger than 96".
Particle Board and MDF can be obtained in larger sizes.

Board Finishes: Melamine, Laminate, Acrylic, Paint and RTF.
Melamine: A durable, cost-effective cabinet finish made by fusing paper soaked in resin to a particleboard or MDF core, offering a smooth, scratch-resistant surface. These are very thin, and need special machinery to apply onto the surface of a board. They come in varied thicknesses, and designs; some can even mimic a woodgrain feel on their surface.
Eg Use cases: Door/Drawer  Fronts, Finish Side Panels, Cabinet interiors.
Laminate: A high-pressure finish made of multiple layers of resin-coated paper, bonded to a substrate, known for its durability, variety of designs, and resistance to stains and moisture. These are much thicker than Melamine because of the added layers, and can be found as separate sheets by themselves, which anyone could purchase and glue onto a surface.
Eg Use Cases: Door/Drawer  Fronts, Finish Side Panels, Countertops, Desktops and other working surfaces.
Acrylic: A glossy, high-end cabinet finish made from acrylic sheets adhered to a substrate, providing a sleek, mirror-like appearance with excellent durability and UV resistance.
Eg Use Cases: Door/Drawer  Fronts, Finish Side Panels. Depending on the quality, it can also be used for Desk tops.
Paint: A versatile cabinet finish applied directly to the surface, allowing for customizable colors and textures, but requiring careful maintenance to avoid chips or scratches.
Eg Use Cases: Door/Drawer  Fronts, and Finish Side Panels.
RTF (Rigid Thermofoil): A vinyl film vacuum-pressed over MDF. This creates a seamless, moisture-resistant finish that mimics the look of wood or paint.
Eg Use Cases: Door/Drawer Fronts.

How Shaker doors are typically made (Not how we make it) (Video)
The reason this type of shaker doors are made like this, is because real wood has 'wood movement', where its size can change based on humidity. The bigger panel that is used in the center of the shaker door actually has space to expand and contract. If not for this, the door can warp and crack overtime.
But we make Shaker doors by carving out the center of a single piece of MDF. MDF is not real wood, and doesn't expand and contract. So it is a popular choice of material for this application. But this is important to note when explaining to customers if they want Shaker doors from us. Some customers prefer to use real wood. So we need to be clear that we do not use real wood when we make shaker doors. 
Flat Panel / Slab Doors
Flat Panel / Slab doors are what we are able to make. The core of these boards are typically made of either particleboard, or MDF. And the Finish could be Melamine, Laminate, or Acrylic. sheets.
These are also the easiest/quickest to work with. The boards usually come prefinished from the suppliers, and all we need to do is cut them to shape, and add an Edge-band strip on the sides to cover the exposed core.
Edge-banding the Boards is important for two reasons:
It seals the exposed sides of the board, and greatly helps resist water damage and exposure to humidity.
Using Edge-banding with a matching color to the surface finish, will help make the board look more uniform all around.

Standard Dimensions we follow when designing cabinets:
For Kitchens
Base Cabinet Box : 24" Deep, and 34.5" High. 
Widths are in increments of 3", starting at 9", and can go up to 42" depending on the type of  Base cabinet used. (Drawers, Just doors, open shelves, etc.)
The Height is 34.5", because most countertops are 1.5" Thick. We don't provide countertops, but this will allow the total height of the cabinet from the ground to the top of the countertop to be 36"H.
For ADA Compliant cabinetry (Used in many commercial locations, and homes designed to accommodate disabilities) , the overall height has to be 34". So our Cabinet box will be 32.5" High.
Wall Cabinet Box: 13" Deep. Heights and Widths will vary.
For Standard Heights and Widths, they are in increments of 3". So 12, 15, 18, 21, 24,… up to 42". 
The most common heights are 36", 42", 30", and 24".
Most Common Widths are 12", 15", and 18" for Single Door Wall Units. And 24", 30", and 36" for Double Door Units.
Tall Cabinet Box: 24" Deep. Heights for this will vary as well. The more common height is around 96".
Most Common Widths are  15", 18" and 21" for Single Door Tall Units. And 24", 30", and 36" for Double Door Units. And 30" or 36" for Oven/Microwave units.
For Bathrooms
Base Cabinet Box: 21" Deep, and either 34.5" or 32.5" High. (ADA compliance applies here as well)
